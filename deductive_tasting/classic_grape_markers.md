## Cabernet Sauvignon

### Left Bank Bordeaux Blend

- deep black fruit
- lean blackcurrent
- earth/forest floor
- bright acidity
- some wines have pronounced herbal and earthiness
- pencil shavings
- use of high quality french oak

### New World: California

- sight: deep ruby
- nose:
  - Ripe to jammy black fruit
  - olive, cedar, green herb, sometimes menthol mint
  - cooler climate more red and herbal notes
  - oak maturation gives smoke, toast, sweet baking spices and sawdust
  - toasted coconut seen in wines with american oak
- palatte:
  - full bodied and dry
- structure:
  - alcohol: medium plus to high
  - acidity: medium to medium plus
  - tannin medium-plus to high

comparison to Bordeaux: riper and richer without savoury earth/mineral component

### New World: South Australia - Coonawarra

- sight: deep ruby
- nose:
  - blackberry, black cherry, blackcurrent fruit
  - pronounced mint/eucalyptus and notes of olive and herbs.
  - ferrous, ironstone, sanguine note
- palate:
  - full bodied and dry
- structure:
  - alcohol: medium-plus to high
  - acidity: medium to medium plus
  - tannin: medium-plus to high

## Pinot Noir

### Burgundy: Côte de Nuits

- sight: light to medium ruby
- nose:
  - red fruit
  - tea, floral, herb, earthy complexity
  * age: gamey-farmyarrd-earthiness
  * oak: smoke, vanilla, sweet spice and wood (premier cru to GCru)
  - bunch: light camphor
- palette:
  - medium to medium + and dry to bone dry
- structure:
  - alcohol: medium to medium +
  - acidity: medium +
  - tannin: medium- to medium
  - bunch: firmer, stalky tannins
  - heavier than wines from CdB

light in color but concentrated in flavor. Light flavoured with birght red fruit, tea-spice, earthy complexity and new oak.

### Burgundy: Côte de Beaune

- sight: light to medium ruby
- nose:
  - tart or ripe red fruits depending on vintage: cherry, raspberry and cranberry, pommegranate in riper years.
  - non-fruit: black tea, rose floral, clay/earth/mineral
  - age add gamey/farmyard/earth
  - oak: vanilla, baking spice, toast.
- structure:
  - alcohol: medium to med+
  - acidity: med+
  - tannin: med- to med
  - lighter than those of CdN (except Pommard)

Earthier and softer in tannin than CdN, less fruit forward, leaner.

### New World: California and Oregon

- sight: medium to deep ruby
- nose:
  - ripe red fruit, blak in warm vintages/regions
  - herb, floral, tea nad more
  - sweet cola
  - oak: sweet spice and wood
  - whole bunch: reduce alcohol and color, increase tannin, add herbal savoury note similar to camphor
- palate:
  - med- to med+, dry
- structure:
  - alcohol: med to med+
  - acidity: med to med+
  - tannin: med to med+

Supple red berry fruit, spices and new wood. Stem tannins often present but a relative lack of earthiness when compared to Burgundy.

### New World: New Zealand

- sight: light to med ruby
- nose:
  - red fruit: fresh and dry
  - nonfruit: herb, floral, tea, mineral/sol
  - oak: smoke, sweet spice, wood
  - whole bunch possible
- structure:
  - alcohol: med to med+
  - acidity: med to med+
  - tannin: med to med+

Similar to California and Oregon fruit, spices and wood however more herbal and chalky than those other new world wines.

## Merlot

### Right Bank Merlot

- sight: deep ruby red
- nose:
  - fruit: red and black, green herb, forest floor, violet floral, earth mineral
  - oak: vanilla, sweet spice, toast/smoke
- palate:
  - med+ to full
  - supple, rich and lush
  - dry to bone dry
- structure:
  - alcohol: med to med+ to high in warm vintages
  - acidity: med to med+
  - tannin: med to med+

Generally similar to left bank wines but soter tannins and more herbal/vegeta character.

### New World: California, Australia and Chile

- Slight: very deep ruby
- Nose:
  - ripe black fruit
  - green herb
  - bitter chocolate
  - oak
  - mint, eucalyptus
- Palatte:
  - full-bodied
  - richer and riper than right bank Bordeaux with newoak
- structure:
  - alcohol: med+ to hight
  - acidity: med to med+
  - tannin: med+ to hight

Keys: generally Merlot tends to be ripe and supple with lush fruit and herbal notes; full-bodied wines display considerable alcohol with a relative lack of earthiness and considerable new oak. Tannins tend to be less angular than Cabernet Sauvignon although Cabernet is often blended with Merlot for added structure.
