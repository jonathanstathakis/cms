# Wine Pairing

## Elements of a Pairing

- Weight:
  - weight of dish match weight of wine OR matched with acidity
- wines can be grouped into 3 main categories based on taste:
  - bitterness: red wines
  - acidity: white, rosé and sparkling
  - sweet: sweet wines
- flavors (food):
  - salt:
    - sweet or fruity wines
    - softens tannins
- flavors (wine):
  - oak can easily overpower food
  - diff. cooking techniques (roasting? smoking?) can offest
- Fruits/Herbs/Vegetables/Spices/Nuts:
  - no strict rules
  - too much green vegetables can cause metallic aftertaset in wine
- texture:
  - tannin:
    - fades with age
    - protein and fat soften tannins
- fats:
  - oil/fat/butter
  - acid in dish can balance tannin in wine (?)
  - crisp wines offer effective contrast
- taste:
  - sweet
  - sour
  - salty
  - bitter
  - heat
- acidity:
  - ultimate contrast
  - balance high acid ingredients
  - highlights core focal point ingredients
  - match acidity in food:
    - if not, wine looks flabby
    - balances sugar
- residual sugar:
  - match or exceed sugar in dish:
    - if not, wine looks bitter
  - counters aromatic heat i.e. chilli - Provencal, Italian, North African
  - can balance salt
- alcohol:
  - balance weight/body
  - is easily accentuated by salt, heat etc.
- when considering the dish, identify its dominant tastes. E.g.:

  - beef wellington:
    - fat
    - umami
    - salty

- key points:
  - weight with weight
  - acid loves and needs acidity
  - fish oils love acidity, hate tannin
  - tannin loves fat, hates fish oil
  - acidity can cut saltiness
  - sweet needs sweet
  - intensity couples intensity
  - alcohol and spice is bad, both are accentuated
  - sugar neutralises spice

## How to Design a Match

1. determine dominant component of dish
2. determine suppporting components
3. consider sauces

- dish 1:
  - dish: Foie gras

- dish 2:
  - dish: goat cheese
  - wine: sauvignon blanc
  - reason: high acid cheese meets high acid wine
- dish 3:
  - dish: steak (beef, lamb, venison)
  - wine: Cabernet Sauvignon
  - reason: tannin meets protein and fat
- dish 4:
  - dish: sardine
  - wine: fino sherry
  - reason: salt and fish meet high acid wine
- dish 5:
  - dish: stilton
  - wine: port
  - reason: salty cheese meets sweet
- dish 6:
  - dish: truffle dish
  - wine: nebbiolo
  - reason: congruent earthy elements (mushroom/truffle)
- dish 7:
  - dish: curry dish
  - wine: Alsace PG / Gewürztraminer
  - reason: aromatic profile and sweetness balances spice

## Pairing Guidelines

- pair reds with bold-flavored meats
- pair whites with light=intensity meats i.e. fish, chicken
- bitterness in wine is balanced with fat
- match the sauce rather than the meat
- white, sparkling and rosé create complementary pairings (few shared characters, increasing the complexity of the overall flavour scape)
- reds tend to create congruent pairings (lots of shared characters)

- consider:
  - salt
  - acid
  - sweet
  - bitter
  - fat
  - spice

## Complementary and Congruent Pairings

- find complementary or Congruent pairings. E.g. mac and cheese:
  - complementary pairing:
    - SB:
      - Acidity
      - fruit
      - floral
      - expands the flavorscape while working well with the fattiness
  - congruent pairing:
    - creamy rich chardonnay
    - character similar to dish, enhances already present character.
- consider distinctive elements, for example smoky gouda or hazelnuts
