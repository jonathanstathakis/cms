# Connecting Food to Wine Options

See [[pairing-options]] for the pool the wine are drawn from.

- Oysters Natural:
  - Trad. Meth. Champage: umami in the Champagne amplifies oyster umami
  - Chablis: fresh crisp citrus-driven, saline-laced Chablis plays the part of a lemon dressing to the oyster, amplifying the seaspray and helping to cleanse the palate.
- Caviar:
  - Champagne: crisp acidity cuts through salty briny intensity. Meth. Trad. champagnes provide a creamy mouthfeel that matches that of the caviar.
- nicoise salad:
  - provencial rosé: delicate fruit complements the salt and bitter elements such as tomato and olives, acid structure to match dressings and avoid overpowering the delicate flavors.
- Foie Gras:
  - Sauturnes or Late Harv RI: rich oily meets creamy rich fat
- Goat Cheese:
  - SB: high acid cheese meets high acid wine
- Steak (beef, lamb, venison):
  - CS: tannin meets protein and fat
- Sardine:
  - Fino Sherry: salt and fish oil meet high acid wine
- Grilled Salmon:
  - new world pinot noir: Salmon is a firm, flavoursome and fatty fish, and when grilled aquires smoky, caramelised elements. A lightly oaked new world Pinot Noir will possess the tannins and minerality to help work through the protein and wash away the fat while the fruit flavour will be amplified by the fat, and the smoky oak elements intensifying the grilling.
- Asparagus:
  - Grüner Veltliner:
    - Classic in Austria
    - crisp acidity masks the green vegetable bitterness.
- Pesto:
  - Sauvignon Blanc/Trebbiano:
    - Green vegetal notes are congruent with the basil while the brilliant acidity cuts through the olive oil while providing citrus and other fruit to complement what is typically a rich, salty dish.
- Shellfish:
  - medium weight white with brilliant minerality and citrus notes matches the body of the shellfish while cleansing the palette of the fish fats.
- seafood:
  - Pinot Grigio:
    - crisp acidity and green apple character complement the fish by playing the role of citrus juices to cut through the fat.
  - Chablis:
- Tomato sauces:
  - Chianti:
    - tomato sauces are high acid foods which require a high acid wine, otherwise the wine will appear flabby. Chianti with its high acidity and rustic herbal aromas matches the acidity in the dish while complementing it with the mentioned notes.
- Coq au Vin:
  - Burgundy Rouge:
    - Chicken is a leaner meat, and as mushrooms and burgundy rouge are used to make the dish, pairing with a lower tannin burgundy rouge is a natural choice, exemplifying the earthiness of the mushroom and red fruit in the stewed wine while providing high acidity and mild tannins to break down the chicken protein.
- Game:
  - Northern Rhône Syrah:
    - Medium tannin wine is appropriate for the lean pheasant and venison meet, and the pepper, smoke and dark berry of the Syrah complement both the meat and the typically herbaceous sauces they are served with.
- truffle:
  - nebbiolo: early elements are congruent
- earthy dishes (mushrooms):
  - Pinot Noir: earthy meets earthy, fruit sweetness provides a complement.
- curry:
  - alsace PG/Gewurtz: aromatics and sweetness balance spice.
- Stilton:
  - port: salt cheese meets sweet wine
- Chocolate:
  - Ruby Port:
    - sweet wine avoids creating a bitter effect by being sweeter than the dessert. The fruit provides a complementary pairing by introducing freshness and organic sweetness alongside the spice and sweetness of the chocolate, while the spice notes amplify those already present.
