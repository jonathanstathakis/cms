## Deductive Tasting

The analysis of a wine is broken into 6 aspects: sight, aroma, palate structure and flavor, initial conclusion and final conclusion.

Sight touches on the appearance of the wine as a solution: is it clear, is it bright, is it concentrated, is there gas, what is the color, how consistent is the color, is there stain, is there legs, etc.

Aroma looks at the intensity and character of the aroma. It can be broken down into two subsections: general and descriptive analysis. The prior assesses the intensity of the nose, ripeness of the fruit character and determination of age. The latter provides a detailed summary of the primary fruit, primary non-fruit, secondary, organic, mineral and tertiary characters.

Palate structure ranks the sweetness from bone dry to dessert, tannin from low to high, acidity from low to high, alcohol from low to high, and body/texture from tart to round.

Palate Flavour like Aroma provides a detailed description of the primary fruit, primary non-fruit, secondary, tertiary characters. It follows with an assessment of the balance: does any element dominate, length/finish: short to long, and complexity: from low to complex.

The conclusive identification of the wine is broken down into Initial Conclusion and Final Conclusion. First a general identification is made: the climate, grape variety/blend, possible countries and age range. After this, and after reviewing the previous notes, a specific conclusion on the vintage, grape variety/blend, country of origin, region/appellation and quality heirarchy is made.
